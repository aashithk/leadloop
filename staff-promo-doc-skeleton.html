The Staff Promo Doc Skeleton

Staff promotions aren't decided on vibes — they're decided on packets. This is the skeleton of the promotion document senior engineers submit when going for staff: the exact sections reviewers look for, what goes in each, and how to phrase scope so it survives calibration. Download it as Markdown, fill it in, hand it to your manager.

The 9 sections your packet needs

  1. Header

    Name, current level, target level, review period, sponsor. Reviewers read dozens of packets — make yours scannable in ten seconds.

  2. Executive summary

    One paragraph: the scope story. "I led X across N teams, delivering Y business outcome."

  3. Business impact

    Outcomes with numbers attached. Impact first, activity second — always.

  4. Scope of influence

    Teams influenced, projects owned, headcount denominator, on-call/ownership footprint.

  5. Technical depth

    The hardest problems you personally solved and the designs you led — your differentiator at staff.

  6. Leadership & mentorship

    People grown, processes you fixed, conflicts you resolved, decisions you drove without authority.

  7. Multi-team evidence

    Cross-org projects, design reviews given, tech talks, standards adopted by other teams.

  8. Peer feedback themes

    Themes from peer reviews — your words would be self-promotion; theirs is evidence.

  9. Growth areas & next cycle

    Two honest gaps and the plan. Strong packets own their gaps — reviewers distrust perfect ones.

Returning after a career break?

This same skeleton works for you — frame by impact and scope, not chronology:

  • Keep pre-break roles in the packet with impact bullets in this exact format; recency matters less than evidence of scope.
  • Contract, consulting, and open-source work during or after the break count as scope evidence — list them as entries, not footnotes.
  • In the executive summary, one line naming the break directly reads as confidence; gaps you hide invite questions you won't control.

Staff Promotion Packet — template

Name
[Your name]
Current level
[e.g. Senior SWE]
Target level
[e.g. Staff SWE]
Review period
[e.g. H2 2025 – H1 2026]
Sponsor
[Manager name]

Executive summary

[One paragraph: the scope story. "I led <project> across <N> teams, delivering <outcome>." Name the single biggest thing you did.]
Write it like this: "Over the review period I led the checkout reliability program across payments, fraud, and platform teams (18 engineers), cutting checkout failures by 40% and unlocking the EU launch."

What reviewers scan for: scope (how many teams/people) and outcome (what changed) in the first three lines.

Business impact

[3–5 bullets. Each: outcome → number → your role. Lead with the result, not the activity.]
  • [Cut p99 latency from X ms to Y ms for <service>, worth ~$<N>/yr in infra savings.]
  • [Shipped <feature> enabling <team/customer> to <outcome>.]
  • [Killed / deprecated <legacy system>, removing <N> on-call pages per quarter.]
Write it like this: "Reduced onboarding drop-off 22% by redesigning the signup data pipeline — my design, my rollout, measured in the A/B."

What reviewers scan for: numbers attached to outcomes. "Led the migration" with no result stated reads as activity, not impact.

Scope of influence

[Make the denominator explicit: teams influenced, engineers affected, services owned, on-call footprint.]
  • [Teams influenced: <list teams, not just your own>]
  • [Engineers whose work I shaped: ~<N> (reviews, designs, mentorship)]
  • [Services owned end-to-end: <list> — on-call, runbooks, capacity]
  • [Ambiguity handled: <project with no clear spec that you defined>]
Write it like this: "My designs were the reference for 4 teams' integrations; I carried the on-call for the platform tier serving all of them."

What reviewers scan for: evidence the scope crosses team boundaries. Single-team scope is the most common reason staff packets stall.

Technical depth

[The hardest problems you personally solved. Name the trade-offs only a senior engineer would navigate.]
  • [Hardest technical problem: <what> — why it was hard: <trade-off>]
  • [Designs I led that others built on: <list>]
  • [Incident where my judgment mattered: <what happened, what you decided>]
Write it like this: "Chose consistent hashing over range partitioning for the hot-key problem — accepted harder rebalancing to avoid the cascading-failure mode range shards hit at peak."

What reviewers scan for: judgment under trade-offs. Staff isn't "more code" — it's harder decisions.

Leadership & mentorship

[Influence without authority: people grown, processes fixed, conflicts resolved, decisions driven.]
  • [Engineers mentored and where they are now: <names optional, outcomes matter>]
  • [Process I fixed: <e.g. incident reviews, deploy pipeline> — before/after]
  • [Disagreement I resolved without authority: <who, what, how>]
  • [Decision I drove that others opposed: <what, why you were right or what you learned>]
Write it like this: "Convinced the mobile and backend teams to share one contract-testing suite — killed a class of integration bugs and a recurring argument."

What reviewers scan for: leadership that doesn't require a title. If every bullet says "my team," the scope story is thin.

Multi-team evidence

[Artifacts other teams adopted: standards, libraries, reviews, talks. This is the staff bar in concrete form.]
  • [Standard/library adopted by <N> teams: <what>]
  • [Design reviews given outside my team: <examples>]
  • [Tech talks / write-ups with cross-team reach: <links>]
Write it like this: "My API versioning guideline was adopted by 6 teams; I reviewed the 3 riskiest migrations outside my org."

Peer feedback themes

[Paste 3–5 short quotes or themes from peer reviews. Attribute by role, not name: "Staff engineer, platform team."]
  • ["<quote>" — <role, team>]
  • ["<quote>" — <role, team>]

What reviewers scan for: other people's words about your impact. One strong peer quote beats a page of self-assessment.

Growth areas & next cycle

[Two honest gaps and what you're doing about each. This section is a strength, not a weakness.]
  • [Gap: <e.g. exec communication> → Plan: <e.g. monthly updates to skip-level>]
  • [Gap: <e.g. ambiguous 0→1 scoping> → Plan: <e.g. leading the <project> discovery phase>]
Write it like this: "My written proposals land; my live exec updates ramble. I'm running the monthly platform review to practice."

What reviewers scan for: self-awareness with a plan. Packets with no gaps read as lacking calibration, not as perfect.
