The System Design Interview Pitch Sheet

Senior candidates don't improvise the 45-minute system design round — they run it. This is the 5-part structure that keeps you in the driver's seat: what to clarify, what to draw, where to go deep, and how to close. Print it, rehearse with it, take it into your prep.

How the 45 minutes break down

  1. Clarify & scope ~5 min

    Nail the requirements before you draw a single box. Interviewers fail candidates who start building the wrong system.

  2. High-level design ~10 min

    Three to five boxes, one write path, one read path. Keep it boring on purpose.

  3. Deep dive ~15 min

    Go deep on two bottlenecks the interviewer steers you toward — data model, caching, consistency.

  4. Scale the numbers ~5 min

    Back-of-envelope math, out loud: QPS, storage, bandwidth.

  5. Trade-offs & close ~5 min

    Name what breaks at 10x, what you'd do with another month, and invite the follow-ups.

Part 1 — Clarify & scope

Ask before you draw

  • Who are the users, and how many? (DAU, peak vs. average)
  • Read-heavy or write-heavy? What's the ratio?
  • Latency target? (e.g. p99 under 200ms)
  • Consistency: can reads be stale, or must they be exact?
  • What is explicitly out of scope for these 45 minutes?
Say it like this: "Before I sketch anything — are we designing for roughly a million daily users with a 100:1 read-write ratio, and is eventual consistency acceptable for the feed?"

Trap to avoid

Jumping to Kafka vs. Kinesis in minute two. Interviewers read that as "builds before thinking" — a senior-level fail signal.

Part 2 — High-level design

Draw boring boxes

  • Client → load balancer / API gateway → stateless services → storage. That's enough.
  • Trace one write end-to-end, then one read. Narrate the data flow out loud.
  • Name your storage per workload (relational for transactions, object store for blobs, cache for hot reads) — one line of justification each.
Say it like this: "Writes go through the API service into Postgres; reads for the hot feed come from a cache layer in front of it. Let me trace a post creation…"

Trap to avoid

Twelve microservices on the whiteboard. Every box is a liability you now have to defend — keep it to what the requirements earn.

Part 3 — Deep dive

Go deep where steered

  • Data model: tables/keys, partition key, and why that key avoids hotspots.
  • Caching: what gets cached, invalidation strategy, thundering-herd guard.
  • Consistency trade-off: name the CAP choice explicitly and what you give up.
  • Follow the interviewer's nudges — the deep dive is theirs to direct, yours to execute.
Say it like this: "I'll partition by user ID with consistent hashing so no single node goes hot, and accept eventual consistency on the feed count — exact counts aren't worth the coordination cost here."

Trap to avoid

Staying shallow everywhere. Senior+ signal is depth on hard trade-offs, not breadth of buzzwords.

Part 4 — Scale the numbers

Do the math out loud

  • QPS: DAU × actions per user ÷ 86,400, then ×3–5 for peak.
  • Storage: writes/day × bytes per record × retention days.
  • Bandwidth: QPS × average response size.
  • Sanity-check every number against your design ("so 3 replicas of 500GB each").
Say it like this: "A million DAU posting twice a day is ~25 writes/sec average, ~120 at peak — trivially handled by one Postgres primary, so the real scaling question is the read fan-out."

Trap to avoid

Silent arithmetic. The math matters less than showing you think in orders of magnitude — narrate it.

Part 5 — Trade-offs & close

End like a senior

  • What breaks first at 10x scale? Name it before they ask.
  • One alternative you considered and rejected — with the reason.
  • "With another month I'd…" — shows judgment about where effort goes.
  • Invite follow-ups: "Happy to go deeper on the consistency model or the cache layer."
Say it like this: "At 10x, the fan-out service becomes the bottleneck — I'd shard it by celebrity-user tier. I considered a pure push model but rejected it for the storage cost on inactive users."

Trap to avoid

Ending with "…and that's it." The close is where staff-level judgment shows — don't trail off, land it.

Rehearsal drill (20 minutes)

  1. Pick a prompt: design a URL shortener, design Instagram feed, or design a rate limiter.
  2. Run the 5 parts with a timer — no pausing.
  3. After: mark every place you said a technology name without a justification. That's your fix list.
