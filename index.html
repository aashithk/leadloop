This is the level you want to be hired at — not necessarily the one you're at now. Google job posts generally use titles rather than internal level labels, so your resume has to prove the scope of the band you're targeting, or the hiring committee will place you lower. Not targeting Google? Leave this on “general resume”. Written from real experience inside Google's hiring process.

Returning after a break or switching careers? Say so in one plain line in your summary (“Career break 2021–2024; previously a backend engineer…”), then lead with what you can do now. About Aashith

Aashith Kamath is a software engineer at Google. He has served on 50+ promotion and hiring committees — including senior and senior-staff leadership decisions — so he has seen exactly what gets engineers hired, promoted, or passed over — from hiring committees to senior-staff promotion decisions, from the inside of the room where it happens. At Google he drives agentic enterprise AI work on Google Chat, so he sees both sides of the AI shift: building the technology, and deciding who gets hired alongside it. Over his career he has grown engineers into tech leaders, set up teams and organizations, and led on both the technical and organizational fronts — from architecture decisions to the people systems that make teams work.

He started LeadLoop because the same engineers who ace hard technical problems often stall on two things nobody teaches: getting past the interview loop, and growing into leadership once they're in. LeadLoop exists to teach both, plainly and from experience.
